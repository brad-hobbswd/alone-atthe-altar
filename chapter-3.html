Chapter Three
What I Could Not Let Go

For My Child

There are some things you can surrender to God without ever surrendering your love for them.

Fatherhood was not something I could separate from who I was. My child was someone I wanted to provide for, protect, guide, and love, so the changes happening around my family affected me deeply. I had spent years believing that if I showed up, worked hard, and did what I believed was right, I could help create stability. Then I faced circumstances that reminded me that a father can give everything he has and still not control the outcome.

A Father's Responsibility

I wanted my child to know that I was there and that my love was steady. When I was away working, I could not see everything that happened, and that helplessness was difficult. I wanted to know that my child was being cared for with patience, love, consistency, and guidance. I could not be everywhere, and I could not make every decision, but I could remain committed to being a father.

As family circumstances changed, I had to learn that being responsible for my child did not mean being able to control every circumstance surrounding my child. That distinction was painful, but it mattered. I could continue to love, to pray, and to do what was right without pretending that I could fix everything.

When Family Began to Change

Eventually, separation became part of our reality. It changed routines and created a kind of distance that I had never wanted for my family. I had questions I could not answer, but one thing remained clear: my love for my child did not disappear because circumstances changed. I still cared, still worried, still prayed, and still wanted the best for my child.

There is a particular kind of pain in loving someone you cannot always protect from every hardship. I had to resist the temptation to turn every difficult circumstance into proof that I had failed. I could examine my own choices honestly, recognize where I could have done better, and still understand that I could not take responsibility for choices that belonged to someone else.

A Father Can Pray

So I prayed for my child. I prayed for protection, wisdom, peace, and the assurance that my child would know they were loved. I prayed when I could not be there and when I did not know what was happening. Prayer became the place where I could remain a father even when I could not physically control the circumstances.

I had wanted action. I wanted something I could put my hands on and fix. Instead, I learned to trust God with the person I loved most. Giving my child to God in prayer did not mean loving my child less. It meant recognizing that God's ability to protect, guide, and love my child was greater than my own.

Love Does Not End at a Distance

Separation changed many ordinary things. There were moments I missed, conversations I wished I could have had, and simple parts of family life that I never expected to miss so deeply. Yet distance could not erase fatherhood. I could not control every door that opened or closed, but I could choose whether I continued to love, and I chose to keep loving.

That was the battle I was learning to fight. Not a battle to control every circumstance, but a battle to remain faithful in the middle of circumstances I could not control. I could be hurting and still be a father. I could cry and still pray. I could be uncertain and still choose faith.

What Fatherhood Taught Me

Fatherhood taught me that love is not measured only by what you can control. Sometimes love means remaining faithful when you cannot change the circumstances. Sometimes it means praying when you cannot be physically present. Sometimes it means trusting God with the person you love most while continuing to do your part.

I had spent years trying to hold my family together. Now I was learning how to hold on to God when I could no longer hold the family circumstances together myself. I did not know then what God would ultimately teach me through that surrender, but I knew that my child remained someone I would continue to love and pray for.

I could not control every circumstance surrounding my child.

But I could keep loving, keep praying, and keep placing my child in God's hands.
