Chapter Three
What I Could Not Let Go

For My Child

There are some things you can surrender to God without ever surrendering your love for them.

Being a father was never a responsibility I took lightly. My child was not simply another part of my life. Being a father became part of who I was, and the desire to provide, protect, guide, and love my child was something I carried seriously. That is why the changes happening around my family were so difficult to understand. I could handle a long workday, financial responsibilities, and being tired. What I struggled to understand was how to remain a father when circumstances were beginning to change things I had always believed would remain.

I had spent years believing that showing up, working hard, and doing everything I could would help create stability for my family. I knew I could not control every circumstance, but I believed I could at least do my part. Then I began facing something no amount of work could solve.

A Father's Responsibility

I wanted my child to know that I was there. I wanted my child to feel loved and protected, and I wanted to be the father who could answer questions, provide direction, and be present when life became difficult. There were moments when concerns about discipline and what was happening in our home made me uncomfortable. I did not believe that being a parent meant simply correcting a child. I believed children needed patience, love, consistency, and guidance.

When I was away working, I could not see everything that happened, and that was one of the hardest parts. A father can want to protect his child with everything in him and still be unable to control what happens when he is not present. That helplessness stayed with me. I would go to work knowing that my responsibilities mattered, but part of my mind remained with my family. I wanted to provide for my child, but I also wanted my child to have the kind of home where love was not something that had to be questioned.

I could not fix every situation. I could not be everywhere, and I could not make every decision. I could only love my child and continue doing what I believed was right.

When Family Began to Change

Eventually, separation became part of the reality I had been trying so hard to prevent. That word carries more weight than it seems to when you first hear it. Separation is not simply the distance between two people. It can change routines, relationships, expectations, and the way a parent experiences ordinary moments.

I had questions I could not answer and circumstances I could not control. I did not know exactly how everything would unfold, and I did not want to pretend that I understood every decision or every part of what was happening. What I did know was that I loved my child, and that did not disappear because circumstances changed. I still cared, I still prayed, I still worried, and I still wanted the best for my child. I still carried the responsibility of being a father even when I could not make the family circumstances what I wanted them to be.

There is a particular kind of pain in loving someone you cannot always protect from every hardship. A parent can carry guilt over things that were never completely within their control. I had to learn the difference between being a father and believing I could control everything around my child. I could be faithful to my responsibility without being able to control the outcome.

The Questions I Carried

There were questions I asked myself again and again. Should I have seen something sooner? Should I have done something differently? Could I have worked less and been more present? Could I have said something that would have changed the direction of our family? Those questions can become heavy when you carry them long enough.

Sometimes a parent can look backward and turn every difficult moment into evidence that they failed. I had to be honest enough to examine my own choices without taking responsibility for choices that belonged to someone else. I was not a perfect husband or father. I made mistakes. I had moments when I could have communicated better, listened better, or responded differently. But acknowledging my imperfections did not mean I had to accept blame for everything that happened.

That distinction mattered because I began to understand that repentance and responsibility are not the same thing as carrying another person's choices. I could ask God to show me where I needed to change without assuming that every broken part of my family was mine to repair. That was difficult because I had spent so much of my life believing that if something was broken, I needed to fix it.

A Father Can Pray

So I prayed for my child. I prayed for protection and wisdom, and I prayed that my child would know love and would never believe that circumstances were their fault. I prayed when I could not be there. I prayed when I did not know what was happening. I prayed when I had questions and when I had no answers. There were times when prayer was the only thing I could do.

That used to feel like weakness to me. I wanted action. I wanted a solution. I wanted something I could put my hands on and fix. But there are moments when prayer is not the last thing you do because nothing else worked. Prayer is the first place you should have gone because the situation was never completely in your hands.

I began giving my child to God in prayer in a way I had never understood before. I had to trust that God's ability to protect, guide, and love my child was greater than my own. That did not make the pain disappear, but it gave the pain somewhere to go.

Love Does Not End at a Distance

Separation changed many things, but it did not change the fact that I was a father. There were moments I missed, conversations I wished I could have had differently, and ordinary parts of family life that I never realized I would one day long for. That is one of the strange things about loss. You do not always miss the extraordinary moments first. Sometimes you miss the ordinary ones. You miss the routine, the little conversations, and simply knowing what was happening in your child's day.

I learned that love does not stop being love because circumstances become complicated. I could not control every door that opened or closed, and I could not control every decision made around me. But I could control whether I continued to love my child, and I chose to keep loving. I chose to keep praying. I chose to keep believing that God knew what I could not know.

There were days when that faith felt strong. There were other days when it felt like I was holding on by the smallest thread, but even then, I kept holding on.

What Fatherhood Taught Me

Fatherhood taught me that love is not measured only by what you can control. Sometimes love means remaining faithful when you cannot change the circumstances. Sometimes it means praying when you cannot be physically present. Sometimes it means trusting God with the person you love most.

It also taught me that a father can be hurting and still be a father. I did not have to hide my pain from God in order to love my child well. I could cry and still pray. I could be exhausted and still believe. I could be uncertain and still choose faith. And I could admit that I did not know what would happen next without giving up hope.

Looking back, I realize that some of my deepest prayers were not prayers for my circumstances to become easy. They were prayers that my child would be protected, that my child would know love, and that God would help me remain faithful no matter what happened around us. Those prayers became part of the story too.

I had spent years trying to hold my family together. Then I had to learn how to hold on to God when I could no longer hold the family circumstances together myself. That was a different kind of battle, and I was beginning to understand that I was not fighting it alone.

I could not control every circumstance surrounding my child.

But I could keep loving, keep praying, and keep placing my child in God's hands.
