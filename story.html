The Story Behind the Book

Alone
at the Altar

Some stories are written because they are remembered.

This one was written because some things stay with you until you finally find the courage to put them into words.

Where It Began

Before There
Was a Book,
There Was Life.

Alone at the Altar did not begin as a book idea. It began in the ordinary and difficult places where life happens. It was shaped by faith, fatherhood, responsibility, loss, questions, and moments that could not simply be explained away.

Some of those moments were beautiful. Some were painful. Some were filled with hope, while others left me standing in places where I did not know what was going to happen next.

What connected them all was the faith that remained when life became more complicated than the answers I had been given.

The Prayers

There Were Things
I Could Say Only
to God.

Not every prayer is spoken in front of a congregation. Some are prayed quietly when everyone else has gone home. Some are prayed in cars, bedrooms, hospitals, empty rooms, and places where no one knows how close you are to breaking.

Those were the prayers that became part of this story.

01 Fatherhood
Something Worth Holding On To

Love Gives
the Story
Its Weight.

Fatherhood changes the way you understand strength. It teaches you that love is not always found in having control. Sometimes it is found in continuing to show up when you are tired, uncertain, afraid, and still trying to understand what comes next.

There are things you would carry without hesitation for the people you love. There are battles you would enter simply because walking away is not an option.

02 Loss

Not Everything
Can Be Held
Together.

There are moments when trying harder does not change what is happening. There are circumstances you cannot control, answers you cannot force, and losses that cannot be repaired simply by wanting them to be different.

One of the hardest lessons in life is learning that letting go is not always giving up. Sometimes it is the moment you finally admit that what you have been carrying was never meant to be carried alone.

The Altar

The Place
Where Pretending
Finally Ends.

The altar became more than a place at the front of a church.

It became the place where I could stop trying to explain everything. The place where I could bring questions without answers, pain without resolution, and prayers that did not sound polished enough for anyone else to hear.

Alone at the altar was not the same thing as being abandoned.

03 The God Who Did

Silence Did Not
Mean He Was
Gone.

There were prayers I did not immediately see answered. There were seasons when I could not understand what God was doing and moments when I wondered how much more I could carry.

But understanding came slowly. Looking back, I began to see something I could not always see while I was living through it.

God had been there.

Not always in the way I expected. Not always according to the answers I wanted. But present in ways that became clearer after I had already walked through the hardest parts.

Why This Story Was Written

Because Someone
Else May Be
Standing There Too.

This story was not written because every question has been answered or because every difficult moment has been neatly resolved.

It was written because someone else may be carrying something they cannot explain. Someone else may be praying a prayer no one around them understands. Someone else may be wondering if silence means they have been forgotten.

If that is where you are, this story is for you too.
