Alone at the Altar

A True Story Of Faith Fatherhood Loss
Before the Book

Some Stories Begin
Long Before They
Are Ever Written.

Alone at the Altar did not begin as a book idea. It began in real moments, in real struggles, and in prayers that were often spoken when no one else was listening.

There were things I believed I could carry. There were things I thought I could fix. There were moments I kept trying to hold together long after they had already begun slipping through my hands.

And somewhere in the middle of all of it, I found myself returning to the one place where I knew I could still be honest.

Sometimes
the place where
you feel most
alone becomes
the place where
God meets you.

The Altar

The Place
I Could Stop
Pretending.

An altar is not always a physical place. Sometimes it is the place inside you where you finally stop pretending that you have everything under control.

It is where the answers you thought you had no longer feel enough. It is where the strength you depended on begins to disappear. It is where you bring the parts of your life you cannot repair and admit that you cannot carry them alone.

For me, that place became the altar.

Faith

Faith Was Not
Always Feeling Strong.

There were seasons when faith did not look like confidence. It looked like getting up again. It looked like praying again. It looked like believing when I could not see how anything was going to change.

Faith became less about having all the answers and more about knowing where to go when I had none.

Fatherhood

Love Gives You
Something Worth
Fighting For.

Fatherhood changed the way I understood strength. It taught me that love can make you vulnerable while also giving you a reason to keep standing.

Some battles are not fought because you are certain you will win them. Some battles are fought because the people you love are worth continuing for.

Loss

Some Things
Cannot Be Held Together.

There are moments in life when you discover that trying harder cannot fix everything.

Loss has a way of exposing the limits of our strength. It reminds us that we cannot control every outcome, protect every relationship, or keep every part of life from changing.

And sometimes the hardest prayer is not asking God to fix what is broken.

Sometimes the hardest prayer is learning how to trust Him with what you cannot hold.

The God Who Did

Silence Never
Meant He Was Absent.

Looking back, I can see moments when I thought God had gone silent. I thought my prayers had disappeared into the air. I thought I was standing alone.

But silence and absence are not the same thing.

The God who heard the prayers nobody else heard was still working in places I could not see. He was still present in moments I did not understand. He was still holding what I could not.

“Even in the silence, He was there.”
This Is Why I Wrote the Book

Maybe You Have
Been Alone at
the Altar Too.

Maybe you have prayed prayers that nobody else knew you were praying. Maybe you have carried things you never knew how to explain. Maybe you have wondered whether God heard you when everything around you became quiet.

This story is mine, but I hope somewhere within it you find enough room to recognize pieces of your own.
