The Story Behind the Book

Alone
at the Altar

Some stories are written because they are remembered.

This one was written because some things stay with you until you finally find the courage to put them into words.

Where It Began

Before There
Was a Book,
There Was Life.

Alone at the Altar did not begin as a book idea. It began in the ordinary and difficult places where life happens. It was shaped by faith, fatherhood, responsibility, loss, questions, and moments that could not simply be explained away.

Some of those moments were beautiful. Some were painful. Some were filled with hope, while others left me standing in places where I did not know what was going to happen next.

What connected them all was the faith that remained when life became more complicated than the answers I had been given.

01 The Years
The Years I Was Trying to Hold It Together

Six and a Half Years Can Hold a Lifetime of Questions.

For six and a half years, I tried to hold together a marriage, a family, a home, my responsibilities, my work, and my faith.

I worked long hours and came home tired, often carrying the weight of everything I was trying to keep together. There were days when the house was not cared for, food had not been prepared, dishes were still waiting, and the children were moving through the house while attention seemed to be somewhere else.

I remember feeling like I was trying to keep everything from falling apart while the person I had built a life with seemed to be running in another direction too quickly.

There were questions I could not answer and circumstances I could not control.

Still, I kept trying.

I kept trying because I loved my child. I kept trying because I believed my marriage was worth fighting for. I kept trying because sometimes love makes you hold on long after you realize you are carrying more than one person should have to carry.

Those years left questions in me that did not disappear simply because the relationship ended.

The Prayers

There Were Things
I Could Say Only
to God.

Not every prayer is spoken in front of a congregation. Some are prayed quietly when everyone else has gone home. Some are prayed in cars, bedrooms, hospitals, empty rooms, and places where no one knows how close you are to breaking.

There were things happening in my life that I could not explain to everyone around me.

So I took them to God.

Those were the prayers that became part of this story.

The Songs That Stayed With Me

When I Could Not Find the Words, There Were Songs.

There are songs that become more than songs. They become markers in your life, connected to places, people, prayers, and seasons you never expected to survive.

For me, two songs by Karen Peck & New River became deeply connected to this part of my story: Hold Me While I Cry and Special Love.

I heard those songs during a season of divorce, separation, abandonment, unanswered questions, and the loss of control over circumstances I desperately wanted to change.

I did not simply hear those songs.
I carried them.

They became part of the emotional landscape of those years. They gave language to feelings I could not always explain and helped me remember what those seasons felt like.

The songs did not explain my story.
They helped me survive it.

02 Fatherhood
Something Worth Holding On To

Love Gives
the Story
Its Weight.

Fatherhood changes the way you understand strength. It teaches you that love is not always found in having control. Sometimes it is found in continuing to show up when you are tired, uncertain, afraid, and still trying to understand what comes next.

There are things you would carry without hesitation for the people you love. There are battles you would enter simply because walking away is not an option.

My child was never just part of the story.

My child was one of the reasons I kept fighting to hold it together.

03 Loss

Not Everything
Can Be Held
Together.

There are moments when trying harder does not change what is happening. There are circumstances you cannot control, answers you cannot force, and losses that cannot be repaired simply by wanting them to be different.

Separation changes the way you understand ordinary life. Things that once seemed permanent can suddenly become uncertain. Questions that once had simple answers can become questions you carry for years.

One of the hardest lessons in life is learning that letting go is not always giving up. Sometimes it is the moment you finally admit that what you have been carrying was never meant to be carried alone.

The Altar

Nobody Knew What
I Was Praying About.

The altar became more than a place at the front of a church.

It became the place where I could stop trying to explain everything. It was where I could bring questions without answers, pain without resolution, and prayers that did not sound polished enough for anyone else to hear.

People could see me praying. They could not see everything I was carrying.

I was not at the altar because everything was fine.

I was there because I needed God.

There were Sundays when I could have prayed for the sick, the lost, or the needs of others, but my heart was so consumed with my marriage and my child that those were the prayers I could barely get past my lips.

I remember standing there wondering how much longer I could carry what nobody else knew I was carrying.

I was alone at the altar, but I was talking to God.

And being alone at the altar was not the same thing as being abandoned.

04 The God Who Did

Silence Did Not
Mean He Was
Gone.

There were prayers I did not immediately see answered. There were seasons when I could not understand what God was doing and moments when I wondered how much more I could carry.

But understanding came slowly. Looking back, I began to see something I could not always see while I was living through it.

God had been there.

Not always in the way I expected. Not always according to the answers I wanted. But present in ways that became clearer after I had already walked through some of the hardest parts.

05 The Questions
What Remained

Some Questions Stay With You Until You Face Them.

I carried questions long after those years were over. I replayed conversations, circumstances, decisions, and moments I wished I could understand differently.

I cannot rewrite six and a half years. I cannot recover every answer. I cannot erase every memory.

But I can tell the truth about what those years did to me and what God taught me through them.

This is not a story about making myself the hero or making someone else the villain.

It is a story about loving, losing, questioning, praying, and continuing.

Most of all, it is about discovering that the moments when I thought I was completely alone were never moments when God had abandoned me.

Why This Story Was Written

Because Someone
Else May Be
Standing There Too.

This story was not written because every question has been answered or because every difficult moment has been neatly resolved.

It was written because someone else may be carrying something they cannot explain.

Someone else may be praying a prayer no one around them understands.

Someone else may be standing at an altar wondering if God hears them.

Someone else may be wondering if silence means they have been forgotten.

I know what that feels like.

That is why I wrote this.

Alone at the Altar

Someone Heard What
Nobody Else Could Hear.

I thought I was alone at the altar.

I was never alone with God.

There were prayers nobody else heard. There were tears nobody else saw. There were questions nobody else could answer.

But God was there.

And that is where this story ultimately leads.

Not to everything being explained. Not to every wound disappearing. But to the realization that even when I could not see what God was doing, He had never stopped being there.
