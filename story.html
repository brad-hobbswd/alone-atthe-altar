The Story Behind the Book

Alone
at the Altar

Some stories are written because they are remembered.

This one was written because some things stay with you until you finally find the courage to put them into words.

Where It Began

Before There
Was a Book,
There Was Life.

Alone at the Altar did not begin as a book idea. It began in the ordinary and difficult places where life happens. It was shaped by faith, fatherhood, responsibility, loss, questions, and moments I could not simply explain away.

For six and a half years, I tried to hold together a marriage, a family, a home, my responsibilities, my work, and my faith. I kept trying because I loved my child and believed what we had built was worth fighting for.

Those years left questions that did not disappear when the relationship ended.

The Prayers

There Were Things
I Could Say Only
to God.

Not every prayer is spoken in front of a congregation. Some are prayed in cars, bedrooms, hospitals, empty rooms, and places where no one knows how close you are to breaking.

There were things happening in my life that I could not explain to everyone around me. So I took them to God.

Those prayers became part of this story.

The Songs That Stayed With Me

When I Could Not Find the Words, There Were Songs.

Some songs become markers in your life. For me, two songs by Karen Peck & New River became deeply connected to this season: Hold Me While I Cry and Special Love.

I heard those songs during divorce, separation, abandonment, unanswered questions, and circumstances I desperately wanted to change.

I did not simply hear those songs.
I carried them.

They gave language to feelings I could not always explain and helped me remember what those years felt like.

01 Fatherhood
Something Worth Holding On To

Love Gives
the Story
Its Weight.

Fatherhood changed the way I understood strength. It taught me that love is not always found in having control. Sometimes it is simply continuing to show up when you are tired, uncertain, and afraid.

My child was never just part of the story.

My child was one of the reasons I kept fighting to hold it together.

02 Loss

Not Everything
Can Be Held
Together.

There are circumstances you cannot control, answers you cannot force, and losses that cannot be repaired simply by wanting them to be different.

Separation changed the way I understood ordinary life. Questions that once seemed simple became questions I carried for years.

Eventually I learned that letting go is not always giving up. Sometimes it is admitting that what you have been carrying was never meant to be carried alone.

The Altar

Nobody Knew What
I Was Praying About.

The altar became more than a place at the front of a church. It became the place where I could stop trying to explain everything and bring questions without answers, pain without resolution, and prayers I could barely put into words.

People could see me praying. They could not see everything I was carrying.

I was not at the altar because everything was fine.

I was there because I needed God.

I was alone at the altar, but I was talking to God.

And being alone at the altar was not the same thing as being abandoned.

03 The God Who Did

Silence Did Not
Mean He Was
Gone.

There were prayers I did not immediately see answered. There were seasons when I could not understand what God was doing.

But looking back, I began to see something I could not always see while I was living through it.

God had been there.

Not always in the way I expected or according to the answers I wanted, but present in ways that became clearer after I had walked through some of the hardest parts.

Why This Story Was Written

Because Someone
Else May Be
Standing There Too.

This story was not written because every question has been answered or every difficult moment has been neatly resolved.

It was written because someone else may be carrying something they cannot explain, praying a prayer no one understands, or standing at an altar wondering if God hears them.

I know what that feels like.

This is not a story about making myself the hero or someone else the villain. It is a story about loving, losing, questioning, praying, and continuing.

Most of all, it is about discovering that the moments when I thought I was completely alone were never moments when God had abandoned me.

Alone at the Altar

Someone Heard What
Nobody Else Could Hear.

I thought I was alone at the altar.

I was never alone with God.

There were prayers nobody else heard. There were tears nobody else saw. There were questions nobody else could answer.

But God was there.

That is where this story ultimately leads. Not to everything being explained, but to the realization that even when I could not see what God was doing, He had never stopped being there.
