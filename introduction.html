Beginning the Story
Introduction

There are songs that become more than songs. They become markers in our lives, tied to places, people, prayers, and seasons we never expected to survive.

Two songs by Karen Peck & New River became especially meaningful to me: Special Love, released in 2009, and Hold Me While I Cry, released in 2005.

Special Love reminded me that the love I needed most could not always come from another person. Its message about needing God's grace, His presence, His guidance, and the kind of love that comes from Him met me in a season when I desperately needed to know that He had not left me.

Hold Me While I Cry reached another place in me. It gave words to the kind of exhaustion that comes when you have tried to be strong for too long. There were days when I did not need another explanation. I needed God to meet me in my weakness, hold me through the pain, and give me strength for another day.

Those songs became part of the soundtrack of a season I could not explain to everyone around me. They were not simply songs I heard or listened to during difficult moments. They became reminders that God was still present when I felt abandoned, still listening when my prayers seemed unanswered, and still holding me when I did not know how much longer I could stand.

For six and a half years, I tried to hold together a marriage, a family, and the hope that things could somehow become whole. I was a husband. I was a father. I was working, carrying responsibilities, and trying to keep moving forward while carrying questions I could not answer.

There were things happening in my home that I did not understand. There were things I could not control. There were moments when I felt as though I was carrying the weight of keeping everything together while watching parts of my family life move further beyond my reach.

Eventually came separation, loss, and questions that had no easy answers. I did not know everything that was happening, and I could not force the truth to come to light simply because I needed it. What I could do was pray.

Church became one of the places where I could lay down what I could not carry by myself. I would listen to the preaching, feel something reach into places I had kept hidden, and find myself at the altar again.

I was alone at the altar,
but I was never alone with God.

People around me could see me praying, but they did not know what I was praying about. They could not see the questions behind the tears or the fear behind the silence. There were times when I could barely explain my own pain, much less ask anyone else to understand it.

I remember standing at that altar when I had nothing left to say except, “God, help me.” I was not there because I had figured everything out. I was there because I had nowhere else to take what was breaking inside me.

This book is not an attempt to make myself the hero or someone else the villain. It is my story as I lived it. It is about marriage, fatherhood, separation, unanswered questions, and the painful realization that sometimes you can give everything you know to give and still cannot control what another person chooses.

It is also about faith.

Not the kind of faith that pretends everything is fine. The kind that keeps praying when nothing makes sense. The kind that walks back to the altar when the answers have not come. The kind that discovers God is present even when the people around you do not know what you are carrying.

The songs did not explain my story. They helped me survive it.

And somewhere between the questions, the tears, the silence, and the prayers nobody else heard, I began to understand something I had needed to know all along.

I thought I was alone at the altar.
I was never alone with God.

Before You Continue

Some stories begin with an answer. Mine begins with a question: what do you do when you have prayed for something for years and still do not understand why it fell apart?
