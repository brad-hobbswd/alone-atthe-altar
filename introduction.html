Beginning the Story
Introduction

There are songs that become connected to seasons of our lives in ways we never expect. Years may pass, and then a familiar voice or melody can bring back memories of where we were, what we were carrying, and the prayers we prayed when we did not know what else to do.

For me, two songs by Karen Peck and New River, Hold Me While I Cry and Special Love, became connected to one of the most difficult seasons of my life. When I hear them, I do not simply remember the music. I remember a time filled with questions, prayer, fatherhood, marriage, loss, and circumstances that I could not control.

For six and a half years, I tried to hold together parts of my life that eventually taught me a painful truth: one person cannot always hold together something that requires more than one person. I carried questions that I could not shake and burdens that I did not know how to explain to the people around me. Even when I was surrounded by others, there were times when I felt completely alone with what was happening inside my own life.

We still went to church, and I still went to the altar. There were moments when the preaching seemed to reach directly into places that I had not told anyone about. People around me did not always know what I was carrying, but God did. There were prayers that no one else heard and questions that I could not always put into words, yet I continued returning to God because I did not know where else to take them.

This is not a story about having every answer. It is the story of a husband and father who tried to hold together what he loved while learning that there were things he could not control. It is a story about unanswered questions, painful separation, faith, and the moments when prayer became the only place I knew to go.

I may have felt alone at the altar, but I was never alone in the presence of God.

This is the story of the prayers nobody heard and the God who did.

A Moment to Reflect

Sometimes the songs that find us become reminders of seasons we survived, prayers we prayed, and moments when God was closer than we realized.
