Chapter Four

The God Who Did

What I Learned at the Altar

God Was There

I spent a long time asking God to change what was happening around me. Eventually, I began to recognize what He was doing within me.

There came a point when I had to stop asking only, “God, when are You going to change this?” and begin asking, “God, what are You teaching me while I am walking through it?”

I had prayed for my marriage. I had prayed for my family. I had prayed for my child. I had prayed when I had words, and I had prayed when I had none. There were things I wanted God to fix that I could not fix myself.

But prayer did not give me control over another person. It gave me somewhere to place what I could not control.

The God Who Stayed

Looking back, I can see that God was present in more moments than I recognized at the time. He was there when I was exhausted. He was there when I was confused. He was there when I sat in church carrying questions nobody around me knew I was carrying.

He was there at the altar.

He was there when I went home with the same questions I had brought with me.

He was there when I did not understand why my prayers seemed to take so long to unfold.

God did not always answer my prayers in the way I expected. He did not make every circumstance disappear simply because I prayed about it. But He kept meeting me in the middle of it.

That mattered.

Sometimes we think the answer to prayer has to be a changed circumstance. Sometimes the answer is that God gives us strength to walk through a circumstance we cannot change.

What Prayer Changed in Me

I began to understand that surrender was not giving up. Surrender was finally admitting that I was never meant to carry what only God could carry.

I could be a husband and still not be able to save a marriage by myself. I could be a father and still not be able to control every circumstance surrounding my child. I could work hard, keep showing up, and do everything I knew to do, yet still face things that were outside my hands.

That realization hurt, but it also brought freedom.

I did not have to become bitter to acknowledge that I was hurting. I did not have to make someone else the villain to tell the truth about what I experienced. I did not have to pretend that everything was fine.

I could simply bring the truth to God.

And God could handle the truth.

The Prayers Nobody Heard

There were prayers I prayed that nobody else heard. Some were spoken quietly. Some were only tears. Some were questions I could not form into a sentence.

God heard them all.

That is one of the greatest things I learned during those years. The value of a prayer is not determined by how many people hear it. A prayer can be whispered in an empty room and still reach the God who sees everything.

I thought I was alone because nobody knew the whole story.

But God knew.

He knew what I was carrying before I ever found the words to describe it. He knew what I was afraid of. He knew what I hoped for. He knew the questions I was afraid to ask.

And He stayed.

The God Who Did

When I look back over six and a half years, I cannot tell the story honestly by saying that everything worked out the way I wanted it to. Some things were lost. Some questions remained. Some circumstances were outside my control.

But I can say this: God did not leave me.

He sustained me when I was tired. He met me when I was praying. He kept calling me back when I was tempted to believe I was completely alone. He taught me that faith is not pretending that pain does not exist. Faith is bringing the pain to God and continuing to trust Him with it.

The altar became more than a place where I asked God to change my circumstances. It became a place where God changed the way I carried them.

That is what I remember now.

Not that I had every answer.

Not that I understood every decision or every loss.

But that God was faithful through every part of the story I could not understand.

I was alone at the altar, but I was never alone with God.

The prayers nobody heard were heard by the God who did.
