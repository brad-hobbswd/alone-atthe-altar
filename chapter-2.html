Alone at the Altar Chapter Two

The Prayers in
the Dark

When I Had No Words

Hold Me While I Cry

There were nights when I did not know what to say. I only knew that I needed God to hear what I could not explain.

By this point, the questions were no longer staying at home. They followed me into my workday, into quiet moments, and into the hours when everything around me became still. I could keep moving. I could work. I could take care of responsibilities. But I could not turn off the questions that kept returning.

What was happening to my marriage? What was going to happen to my family? Was there something I was missing? Was there something I should have recognized sooner? I did not have answers to those questions, and sometimes I did not even know who I could ask.

There are moments in life when explaining your pain becomes almost as difficult as carrying it. I did not always know how to put what I was feeling into words. I could not give someone a simple explanation for everything happening inside me because I was still trying to understand it myself.

So I prayed.

Sometimes my prayers were long. Sometimes they were only a few words. There were times when I knew exactly what I wanted to ask God, and there were other times when I could only sit quietly and hope He understood what I could not say.

I wanted God to fix what was broken. I wanted Him to change hearts, restore what was being lost, protect my family, and give me a clear answer about what I was supposed to do. I wanted the uncertainty to end.

But prayer did not always give me an immediate answer.

That was difficult for me.

I believed God could change circumstances, yet I was learning that believing God can do something and understanding why He has not done it yet are two very different things.

When I Could Not Be Strong

Hold Me While I Cry became especially meaningful during that season. The song gave language to something I was struggling to admit. I was tired. I was hurting. I wanted to be strong, but there were moments when I simply did not feel strong anymore.

I had spent so much time trying to hold everything together that I had forgotten I was allowed to admit that I was hurting too.

I did not need to pretend before God. I did not need to make my prayers sound strong or spiritual. I could come to Him exhausted, confused, disappointed, and afraid of what might happen next.

Sometimes the most honest prayer was simply admitting that I needed Him to hold me.

That changed something in me. I began to understand that weakness did not mean I had stopped believing. Crying did not mean I had stopped trusting God. Needing help did not mean I had failed.

It meant I had reached the end of what I could carry by myself.

And that was where I needed God most.

Prayers Nobody Heard

There were prayers I never told anyone about.

I prayed about my marriage. I prayed about my child. I prayed for wisdom because I did not know what decision was right. I prayed for patience when I was exhausted. I prayed for peace when my mind would not slow down.

Most of all, I prayed for God to intervene in places where I could not.

I could not make another person choose differently. I could not control what happened when I was not there. I could not force answers to questions that belonged to someone else. I could only bring those things before God and trust Him with what I could not reach.

That was one of the hardest lessons of those years. There is a difference between responsibility and control. I could be responsible for how I loved, how I worked, how I treated my child, and how I responded to what was happening. But I could not control another person's choices.

I had to learn to give God what I could not fix.

That sounds simple when you say it in a church service. Living it when your family is hurting is something entirely different.

The Altar Became My Place

Church became one of the places where I could finally stop pretending that I had everything under control.

I could sit in a service and listen to the preaching while carrying an entire conversation with God inside my heart. Sometimes a message would touch something I had not told anyone. Sometimes a simple sentence would stay with me long after the service ended.

Then there were the altar calls.

I would go forward because I needed somewhere to put what I was carrying. The altar was not a performance for me. It was a place of surrender.

I prayed for my family. I prayed for my marriage. I prayed for my child. I prayed for strength to keep going and wisdom to know what to do next.

There were times when I could not even explain to myself why I was crying. I simply knew that I was hurting, and I knew where I needed to take it.

I was alone at the altar, but I was talking to God.

People could see the outside of the moment. They could see someone kneeling, praying, crying, or simply remaining there after others had returned to their seats. What they could not see was the story behind the tears.

They did not know every question I had carried into the building. They did not know how many times I had asked God to protect my family. They did not know how often I wondered whether things would ever become whole again.

God knew.

When God Seemed Quiet

There were seasons when I wondered why God seemed quiet.

I was praying, but the circumstances did not immediately change. I was asking for direction, but I did not always receive the clarity I wanted. I wanted God to tell me what was coming, but He did not give me a complete picture of the road ahead.

That kind of waiting can test you.

It can make you wonder whether your prayers are reaching heaven. It can make you question whether God sees what you are seeing. It can make you look at the same situation again and again and wonder why nothing seems to be moving.

But I began learning that God's silence is not the same thing as God's absence.

He was still there when I did not have an answer.

He was still there when I went home with the same questions I had brought to church.

He was still there when I cried.

He was still there when I was angry, tired, confused, and afraid.

And He was still there when I did not know what to pray next.

Looking back, I can see that God was doing something in me that I could not recognize at the time. I wanted Him to change everything around me. He was also teaching me to trust Him with everything I could not change.

Prayer became less about giving God instructions for how I wanted my life to turn out and more about surrendering the outcome to Him.

I still asked Him to restore my family. I still asked Him to protect my child. I still asked Him to make a way where I could not see one. But I was slowly learning that faith does not mean knowing how the story will end.

Sometimes faith means continuing to pray while you do not know.

Sometimes faith means getting back up when the answer has not come.

Sometimes faith means going to the altar again.

And sometimes faith means simply saying, "God, I need You."

I Kept Coming Back

I did not have everything figured out. I did not know what the next year would bring, and I did not know how much more I would have to walk through. I only knew that I could not walk through it without God.

So I kept praying.

I kept going to church.

I kept going to the altar.

I kept listening to the songs that reminded me that God was near.

I kept asking God to help me become the man, husband, and father He wanted me to be, even while I was trying to understand what was happening around me.

The prayers did not always change my circumstances immediately. But they changed where I carried those circumstances.

I stopped trying to carry everything alone.

I began placing it in God's hands, even when I was afraid of what those hands might ask me to release.

I did not know it then, but these prayers were preparing me for another part of the story. There would come a time when prayer would not simply be about trying to save a marriage or hold a household together. It would become about learning how to remain a father through loss, separation, and circumstances I could not control.

That chapter of the story would hurt in a different way.

But before I could learn how to walk through that loss, I had to learn how to bring my fear, my questions, and my tears to God.

I had to learn that I could be honest with Him.

I had to learn that I could cry in His presence.

I had to learn that I could come to the altar without an answer.

And I had to learn that even when nobody else understood the prayer, God did.

I thought I was praying because I needed God to change my circumstances.

I was also learning that God was changing me in the middle of them.
