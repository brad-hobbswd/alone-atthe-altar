Alone at the Altar Chapter One

Trying to Hold
It All Together

For a long time, I believed that if I worked hard enough and remained committed enough, I could keep my family together. I understood that marriage, raising children, working, paying bills, and building a home required sacrifice, and I was willing to make those sacrifices because I loved my family and believed that what we were building was worth fighting for.

Much of my life revolved around work and home. I worked long hours because providing for my family mattered to me, and I took that responsibility seriously. By the time I returned home, I was often tired, but the responsibilities of the day did not disappear when I walked through the door.

There were still children to care for and things around the house that needed attention. There were days when I came home to a house that had not been cleaned, food that had not been prepared, and dishes that remained undone. The children needed attention while I was trying to move from a long day at work into another set of responsibilities at home.

I did not expect perfection. Every family has difficult days, and raising children can be exhausting. What became increasingly difficult for me was the feeling that I was carrying more responsibility while becoming less certain that we were carrying it together.

At the same time, questions about my marriage and the direction of our lives were beginning to grow. Some came from situations I witnessed, while others came from a growing uncertainty about things I did not fully understand. I did not have clear answers, but I could not easily put the questions aside.

I was a husband and a father, and I believed my responsibility was to keep showing up and do everything I could to provide stability for my family. When there was a problem, I looked for a solution. When there was tension, I wanted to find a way to bring peace back into our home.

Looking back now, I can see that I had begun to believe that if I could just do enough, I could somehow prevent everything from falling apart.

What I did not understand then was that one person can be completely committed to saving something and still be unable to save it alone.

There were also things happening in our home that concerned me, particularly when it came to discipline and situations involving the children. As a father, I wanted to know that my child was being cared for and treated with love and patience. There were moments when I felt uncomfortable with situations I could not fully control, especially when I was away working.

I wanted to provide for my child, but I also wanted to be present. I wanted to protect my child, but I could not control every circumstance that took place when I was not there. That helplessness was difficult to carry.

My marriage was also becoming increasingly difficult for me to understand.

I do not want to pretend that I knew everything that was happening, because I did not. There were things I questioned and things I could not shake, but I did not always know whether my concerns would ever be answered.

I could go to work, take care of responsibilities, and focus on what needed to be done, but the questions returned during the quiet moments. I was trying to provide for my family while wondering whether the family I was trying to protect was moving in a direction I could not stop.

I held on because I loved my child and because I had made a commitment. I did not want to walk away from my family, and I did not know what else to do except continue trying.

Eventually, I began asking myself whether there was something I should have done differently. Could I have worked harder, been more patient, or recognized something sooner?

When something important in your life begins to fall apart, it is natural to search for the moment when you could have changed the outcome. The problem is that we sometimes take responsibility for choices that belonged to someone else.

That became especially clear when we went to church.

There were times when I would sit in a service carrying burdens that no one around me knew about. From the outside, I was simply attending church with my family and listening to the preaching. Inside, I was carrying questions about my marriage, concerns about my child, and the exhaustion of trying to keep everything together.

There were times when the preaching seemed to reach into places I had never explained to anyone. The preacher did not necessarily know what was happening in my life, and the people sitting around me did not know the questions I had been carrying. Yet being in the presence of God made it difficult to continue pretending that I was not hurting.

Sometimes I found myself at the altar because that was the only place I knew to take what I was carrying.

I believed in praying for others, but there were seasons when my own heart was so consumed by my marriage and my child that those were the burdens I could not seem to put down.

I prayed because I loved my family and because I did not know what else to do with the questions. There were circumstances I could not control, and somewhere inside me, I still believed that God could do what I could not.

At that point, I was still fighting for what I loved. I was still trying to understand what I could not understand and believing that if I kept showing up and doing my part, there had to be a way through.

What I could not yet see was that while I was fighting to keep everything around me from falling apart, I was slowly beginning to lose the strength to hold myself together.
