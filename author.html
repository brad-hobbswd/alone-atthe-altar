The Author

Bradley Hobbs

Some stories are imagined.
This one was lived.

Alone at the Altar was written from the places where faith, fatherhood, loss, and unanswered questions met.

Behind the Story

I Am More Than
the Things
I Have Survived.

My name is Bradley Hobbs, and I am the author of Alone at the Altar: The Prayers Nobody Heard and the God Who Did.

I did not write this story from a distance. I wrote it from inside it.

These pages were shaped by seasons that asked more questions than they answered. They were shaped by the responsibilities of fatherhood, the weight of loss, the things I wanted to protect, and the painful realization that I could not hold everything together alone.

Through it all, faith remained.

What Matters Most

The Things That
Shape My Story.

01

Faith

Faith has carried me through seasons where I did not understand the road, but I continued to believe that God was still present on it.

02

Fatherhood

Fatherhood has given my life some of its deepest love, greatest responsibility, and strongest reasons to keep moving forward.

03

Family

The people we love become part of every story we tell, even when the most important parts of that story are never spoken aloud.

04

Hope

Hope is not pretending that pain never happened. It is believing that pain does not have the final word.

Why I Write

Because Sometimes
the Things We Cannot
Say Out Loud
Need Somewhere to Go.

Writing became a place where I could slow down long enough to tell the truth about what I had carried.

Not every story is written because the author has figured everything out. Sometimes a story is written because someone else may recognize a part of themselves in it.

If these pages remind even one person that they are not alone, then telling this story was worth it.

To the Reader
A Personal Note

I Do Not Know
What You Have
Been Carrying.

I do not know which prayers you have prayed in private or which questions you have been afraid to ask.

I do not know what you have lost, what you are fighting for, or what you are trying to hold together.

But I hope that somewhere in this story you find a reminder that being alone does not mean you have been abandoned.

Sometimes the most important thing we can discover is that God was there, even when nobody else knew what we were going through.
